This project is the core of my Ph.D. thesis, *Human Action Recognition in Video Sequences using Bio-inspired Models*. It starts from how the visual system processes motion, with direction-selective responses and spatiotemporal receptive-field correlations, and asks whether the same principles can drive practical video understanding.

## Questions
- Can a feedforward network of spiking neurons delineate the boundaries of moving objects from neuron firing rates alone?
- Which spiking neuron models train best with surrogate-gradient techniques?
- How do spiking networks compare with conventional deep models for human action recognition and optical flow?
- Which temporal-integration mechanisms matter most for event-based trajectory prediction?

## Approach
Biophysically grounded neuron models (such as Hodgkin-Huxley) and correlation-based motion paradigms are combined with modern training methods, then evaluated on dynamic visual sequences and event-camera data.
